Travel & stay

Getting there

Flights

Most guests will fly into Benito Juárez International Airport (MEX / AICM), which has direct flights from most major U.S. cities. March is beautiful here and the city fills up fast, so book early.

To get to your hotel, take an authorized airport taxi from an official kiosk, or step outside for an Uber pickup. Skip the drivers who approach you inside the terminal.

Passport & visa

You'll need a valid passport book to fly into Mexico — a passport card or REAL ID isn't enough for air travel. No visa is required for tourist stays under 180 days.

When you land you'll get a tourist entry permit (an FMM), usually issued digitally at the airport. Hang on to any paperwork or QR code you receive, since you may need to show it when you leave.

If you're not a U.S. citizen, requirements vary by country — please check your own country's entry rules well ahead of time.

Where to stay

Neighborhoods we'd point you to

Walkable · food

Roma Norte

Tree-lined streets, gorgeous Art Nouveau buildings, and some of the city's best restaurants and coffee shops — all very walkable.

Leafy · lively

Condesa

Laid-back and green with a park at its center, popular with a younger crowd for its cafés, bars, and Art Deco architecture.

Cobblestones · Frida

Coyoacán

A colorful, small-town feel in the middle of the city: cobblestone plazas, Frida Kahlo's Casa Azul, and lively weekend markets. A bit farther from Centro Histórico, but worth a day trip even if you don't stay there.

Upscale · quiet

Polanco

Polished and upscale, home to Michelin-level restaurants, museums, and high-end shopping — a great pick if you want a quieter, more luxurious base.

Closest to us

Centro Histórico

The neighborhood the venue is actually in. Packed with history, museums, and the Zócalo — ideal if you want to walk to the celebration.

Before you fly

Good to know

Weather & what to pack

March is dry season in Mexico City, so expect:

  • Sunny, warm days (upper 70s to low 80s °F)
  • Cool mornings and evenings (down to the 50s °F)
  • Very little rain

Pack layers: a light jacket or sweater for evenings, comfortable shoes for cobblestone streets, and sunscreen for daytime.

A note on altitude

Mexico City sits at about 7,350 feet (2,240 m) above sea level — higher than Denver. Some guests feel more tired, short of breath, or get headaches for the first day or so. A few easy ways to adjust:

  • Drink extra water starting the day you arrive
  • Go easy on alcohol your first night
  • Take it slow with physical activity on day one

Money

The currency is the Mexican peso (MXN). Cards are widely accepted at restaurants and hotels in Centro Histórico, Roma, and Condesa, but it's smart to carry some cash for taxis, small shops, and tips.

Getting around

Uber works well in Mexico City and is generally the easiest and safest option if you don't know the city. Official pink-and-white taxis are also a good bet — just make sure they're marked and licensed.
